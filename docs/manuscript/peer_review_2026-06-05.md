# Peer review — *Citation-grounded retrieval for faithful LLM somatic variant interpretation*

**Reviewer report**
**Date:** 2026-06-05
**Target tier:** JAMIA / npj Digital Medicine / Bioinformatics-class
**Manuscript reviewed:** `docs/manuscript/preprint.md` (current state)

---

## Recommendation: **Minor revision**

This is a careful, unusually self-disciplined manuscript, and the revision has materially closed the gaps that drove the previous Major-revision decision. The single most important earlier hazard — that a reader would walk away crediting the LLM system with 0.843 accuracy and with empirically *measured* perfect faithfulness — has been substantively fixed: the 0.843 is now repeatedly and explicitly attributed to a deterministic rule engine that is "not the LLM," the faithfulness scorecard is now fenced as gate-enforced-by-construction with the *empirical* pre-gate behaviour (0/106 out-of-bundle citations vs 46.4% in the no-retrieval arm, CI excluding zero) led first, and the accuracy claim for the generative arm is honestly stated as not-significantly-different. CIs are attached to the headline rule-arm and OncoVI numbers, the provisional adjudication is clearly marked as AI-assisted and not pathologist-signed, and a TRIPOD-AI/STARD paragraph, variant-flow, model card, and per-gene table have been added. The remaining work is genuinely minor and largely framing-level. The single most decision-critical remaining issue is one of *paper architecture rather than evidence*: the manuscript still bundles three quite different artifacts — a faithful generative interpreter, a competitive deterministic VICC engine, and a specified-but-unrun future benchmark — under one faithfulness-first banner, and the reader must do real work to keep the strong rule-arm result from lending borrowed credibility to the bounded generative result. The fixes are in place locally; what remains is to make the global takeaway impossible to misread.

## Summary of the manuscript

The authors present a dual-mode somatic variant interpreter that classifies variants on two standards (ClinGen/CGC/VICC oncogenicity and AMP/ASCO/CAP clinical tier) using hybrid citation-grounded retrieval (structured lookups, dense retrieval over CIViC, and a knowledge graph) under a "faithfulness contract" requiring every assertion to cite a retrieved identifier and to abstain to VUS when no variant-specific evidence exists. They compare a Generative mode (the LLM assigns labels), a Deterministic mode (a rule assigns the label and the LLM only writes the rationale), a no-retrieval baseline, and — reported as a separate arm — a citable VICC point-based rule engine. Evaluation uses a 56-variant five-cancer TCGA cohort and the external 70-variant Horak et al. (2022) expert gold standard, with leakage-free faithfulness metrics, leakage controls (temporal holdout, leave-own-evidence-out, second model, embedding ablation), bootstrap CIs, and exact McNemar tests. The central claims are that retrieval makes the generative interpreter faithful and auditable by construction and suppresses confident parametric over-calling, that the generative arm's accuracy is not significantly different from the baselines, and — separately — that the deterministic VICC rule arm reaches 0.843 three-class accuracy on Horak-70, statistically indistinguishable from OncoVI at markedly higher specificity.

I spot-checked the released `horak_vicc.json` and `oncovi_headtohead.json`: the 0.843/0.841 rule-arm figures, the OncoVI 0.797, the McNemar p = 0.66, the specificity contrast, and the discordant-pair counts all reconcile with the committed artifacts. Reproducibility is a genuine strength of this submission.

## Major points

**1. The faithfulness reframing is now correct, but the paper should own the logical consequence more plainly.** The revision rightly separates the *empirical* pre-gate finding (the retrieval arm emitted 106 citations, 0 out-of-bundle, with 18.9% source-category imprecision) from the *by-construction* post-gate scorecard (hallucination 0, grounding 1.0, abstention recall 1.0), and §3.1 states outright that "a system that refuses to emit any non-retrieved identifier will score 0 fabrication and 1.0 grounding by definition." This is exactly the honest framing the previous review asked for, and it is well done. The residual issue is that the abstract and several figure captions (Figure 1c, Figure 2c) still present the four 1.0 values as a "scorecard" / "contract behaviours … all 1.0" without the by-construction qualifier travelling with them. A reader who sees only the abstract and figures — which is most readers — can still come away believing four perfect faithfulness metrics were measured. The cheapest sufficient fix is to make the one genuinely empirical, falsifiable number (pre-gate 0/106 out-of-bundle vs no-retrieval 46.4%, CI excluding zero) the headline faithfulness statistic *everywhere* it is summarised, and to label the 1.0 quartet as "contract guarantees (enforced, not measured)" in the abstract and both captions, exactly as the body text now does.

**2. The generative arm's contribution rests almost entirely on one comparison, and that comparison is underpowered by the authors' own analysis.** The faithfulness-vs-no-retrieval gap (0% vs 46.4%) is the study's load-bearing empirical result, and it is robust. But the *interpretive* weight placed on it — that retrieval "suppresses confident parametric over-calling" in a clinically meaningful way — leans on the 12/56 (21.4%) over-call→VUS reclassification, whose correctness the authors correctly decline to assert. The newly added provisional adjudication (2 of 3 beyond-rules cases favour the rule, not the LLM's abstention) is commendably candid, but it cuts against the framing: it suggests several of those 12 may be over-conservative abstentions rather than corrections. The manuscript handles this honestly in prose, yet the abstract still lists the 21.4% as a positive ("retrieval reclassified 12 … to evidence-grounded VUS") without the counter-signal. I am not asking for new experiments — the authors have correctly deferred adjudication to a powered study. I am asking that the abstract and Discussion principal-findings paragraph carry the provisional adjudication's direction (that the reclassifications may include over-abstentions) so the "suppresses over-calling" claim is not read as a net-positive accuracy/safety effect it has not been shown to be.

**3. The architecture of the paper still lets the strong rule-arm result subsidise the bounded generative result.** This is the most decision-critical remaining issue and it is structural, not factual. Section by section the attributions are now correct — the 0.843 is fenced as deterministic-not-LLM in the abstract, §3.3, Table 1, and the Discussion. But the paper's *title*, framing ("faithful LLM … interpretation"), and narrative arc are about the LLM system, while the only competitive accuracy result belongs to a rule engine the authors explicitly decline to claim as novel (because OncoVI already does criterion-to-source mapping). The reader is therefore left with a paper whose headline number (0.843, indistinguishable from state-of-the-art) is contributed by its least novel component, and whose novel component (faithful generative interpretation) is explicitly not an accuracy advance. This is defensible and honestly disclosed, but it is a hard story to land. I recommend the authors decide, in the framing, which paper this is: (a) a faithfulness-and-auditability methods contribution for LLM variant interpretation, with the VICC engine as a supporting "the framework can be accuracy-competitive" exhibit kept firmly secondary; or (b) a two-contribution paper that gives the rule engine equal billing in the title/abstract. The current text hovers between the two. Option (a) is the cleaner and more honest framing given the novelty disclosure, and would require only tightening the abstract and Discussion so the rule-arm result is consistently positioned as "the same auditable framework can also reach competitive accuracy" rather than as a co-headline.

**4. Parametric leakage remains uncontrolled — adequately disclosed, but the framing should not over-rely on the controls that *are* run.** The authors are explicit (§3.7, Limitations) that the temporal-holdout and LOVO controls govern only the retrieval-corpus channel and that the LLM's own parametric leakage is quantified solely by the no-retrieval floor. This is the right disclosure. My concern is that the abstract's third sentence ("whether any accuracy effect is genuine or a leakage artifact") and the leakage-control narrative can read as though leakage has been broadly neutralised, when in fact the channel most relevant to famous driver variants (parametric recall) is the one channel left uncontrolled. Since there is no generative accuracy effect to defend anyway (Major point 2/3), the cleanest fix is to soften the leakage framing to match: the controls establish that the *faithfulness* behaviour and the absence of a generative accuracy effect are not corpus-leakage artifacts, full stop. The proposed clean ClinVar-2024 temporal holdout (Discussion) is the right future instrument for the parametric channel; it should be named as such (the control that addresses the currently-uncontrolled channel), not merely as "additional benchmark."

**5. The OncoVI head-to-head is fair but is scored on OncoVI's home set, and the safety-profile argument deserves one explicit caveat.** The authors disclose that the comparison is on Horak-70, "OncoVI's own home set," and frame the parity-at-higher-specificity result as a head-to-head on identical variants rather than an independent benchmark — appropriately. The substantive claim that an abstention-first engine with 9 honest VUS calls is "safer" than one with 9 over-calls is well argued and clinically sensible. One caveat is missing: the safer-error-profile argument assumes the operating cost of a missed oncogenic call (the 9 true-oncogenic variants the engine abstains on, which OncoVI catches) is lower than the cost of an over-call. In a setting where a VUS abstention means a patient does not receive an available targeted therapy, that asymmetry is not self-evident. The manuscript already concedes the sensitivity gap and offers the dial; it should add one sentence acknowledging that "safer" is conditioned on the clinical assumption that over-treatment harms more than under-detection in this workflow, which is plausible for a draft-and-review assistant but not universal.

## Minor points

1. **Abstract length and front-loading.** The Results paragraph of the abstract is a single ~250-word sentence-dense block carrying pre-gate faithfulness, post-gate guarantees, generative non-significance, the rule arm, the OncoVI head-to-head, and the leakage controls. For the target venues this will be over the word limit and is hard to parse. Consider leading with the one empirical faithfulness number and the rule-arm headline, and moving the leakage-control enumeration to a single closing clause.

2. **"Not significantly different" vs "non-inferior."** The Objective and Discussion are careful to say the authors report overlapping CIs and non-significant McNemar rather than a formal non-inferiority test because no margin was pre-specified. Good. But the word "comparable" still appears alongside "not significantly different" in several places; "comparable" can be read as a weak equivalence claim. Prefer "not significantly different (no margin pre-specified; this is absence of evidence of a difference at n = 70, not evidence of equivalence)."

3. **Self-consistency at temperature 0.4 vs determinism claim.** The model card lists temperature 0 "for determinism" but 0.4 on voting passes. Since the primary generative numbers depend on n = 3 voting at 0.4, the single-run results are not strictly deterministic, which slightly tensions the "reproducibility" framing. A one-line note on run-to-run variance of the voted call (or a fixed seed for the sampling) would close this.

4. **Provisional adjudication, n = 3.** The beyond-rules adjudication covers only 3 variants and is AI-assisted; this is clearly labelled. Ensure the abstract-level mention (if any is added per Major point 2) states the n = 3 so it is not read as a systematic review.

5. **Figure 5 capability landscape.** The caption correctly says positions are "not a head-to-head on identical inputs" and that numeric cells come from each source study. This is the right disclaimer; consider also stating that the accuracy figures are therefore not directly comparable across rows, since a landscape table invites exactly that comparison.

6. **Per-gene table (S1) PTEN row.** Generative-arm PTEN accuracy is 0.00 (n = 6) — a striking and honest disclosure. A single clause noting that all six PTEN misses are the over-abstention pattern (not random error) would help the reader connect it to the VUS-over-resolution weakness rather than reading it as a coverage bug.

7. **ClinVar generalisation set framing.** The n = 458 ClinVar result is honestly scoped to "oncogenic detection, not full three-class" (benign recall 0, balanced three-class accuracy 0.505). Good. The AUROC 0.902 should not appear in any summary without the adjacent "oncogenic-vs-rest, not three-class" qualifier, as it does correctly in §3.5 but could be lost if surfaced elsewhere.

## Reporting-guideline note

The applicable instruments are correctly identified: **TRIPOD-AI** for the prediction-model reporting and a **STARD** diagnostic-accuracy lens for the external accuracy evaluations against an expert reference standard (a TRIPOD-AI/STARD paragraph, variant flow, and model card are now present, which addresses the previous review's main reporting gap). Remaining substantive asks, as guideline items rather than QA:
- **STARD intermediate-result handling** is addressed in principle (VUS/abstention treated as a reported class). Make the STARD-style variant flow a small flow diagram or explicit count chain in the main text rather than prose, so the 398 → 56 (cohort) and 70 / 69 / 458 (external) paths are auditable at a glance.
- **TRIPOD-AI sample-size justification** is declared as absent-and-deferred, which is acceptable for a non-powered study, but the Discussion's power analysis (several hundred variants for a 0.05 effect) should be cross-referenced from the Methods "Reporting" paragraph so the declaration and its remedy sit together.
- **TRIPOD-AI subgroup reporting** is satisfied by the per-gene Table S1; consider noting the thin-coverage genes (EZH2, PTEN) as a pre-stated subgroup limitation rather than only post hoc.
- No CONSORT/PRISMA/ARRIVE instrument applies (no trial, systematic review, or animal work).

## Confidential comments to the editor

This is a strong revision of a paper that, in its prior form, courted a real over-claiming problem (a perfect-faithfulness scorecard and a 0.843 accuracy that a casual reader would attribute to the LLM). The authors have done the right thing rather than the convenient thing: they have fenced the gate-enforced metrics as guarantees-by-construction, led with the one falsifiable faithfulness number, repeatedly and explicitly attributed the 0.843 to a deterministic rule engine they decline to claim as novel, added CIs and a non-pathologist-signed provisional adjudication, and specified (not run) the powered study needed for any accuracy claim. The core scientific claims now match the evidence, and the released artifacts reproduce the headline numbers on spot-check.

The core claim — that citation-grounded retrieval can make an LLM variant interpreter faithful and auditable by construction, and that this can be done without sacrificing accuracy relative to an open state-of-the-art engine within the same framework — survives revision and is supportable as written. My remaining concerns are framing, not integrity: the paper bundles a novel-but-bounded contribution (faithful generative interpretation, no accuracy gain) with a competitive-but-non-novel one (the VICC rule engine), and a reader must work to keep them apart. I do not think this rises to Major revision, because every required disclosure is now present in the body text; the fixes are abstract/caption/Discussion tightening to prevent misreading, plus a few one-line caveats (parametric-leakage scope, the "safer" clinical assumption, the over-abstention direction of the provisional adjudication). I would be comfortable accepting after a Minor revision and would not require re-review by a second referee, though I would want an editor to confirm the abstract and figure captions carry the by-construction qualifier in the final version. The honesty of the statistical reporting is, for this venue tier, a point in the paper's favour and should be recognised rather than penalised as "negative results."
